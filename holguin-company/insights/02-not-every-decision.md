# Not every decision needs the same approval

*By Adrian A. Holguin, Principal, Holguin & Company · holguincompany.com/principal*

A reversible decision with a limited cost can move with a named owner and a review date. A decision that commits the company to a long contract, a new location, or a different operating model needs a different level of scrutiny.

When both travel through the same process, small decisions wait and large decisions lose the attention they need.

## Sort by three questions

**Can it be undone?** If the decision can be reversed in a week at little cost, the risk of deciding wrong is smaller than the cost of deciding slowly. Give it an owner and a date to review the result.

**What is the exposure?** Dollar amount, contract length, people affected. A decision under a threshold the owner sets, say one percent of annual revenue, does not need the owner.

**Who has the authority?** If the answer is "nobody but me," the company has one approver and a line behind him. Decision rights written down, by role, are the single cheapest operating improvement most owner-led companies never make.

## What the leadership meeting looks like afterward

The weekly agenda has two decision lists. The first is informational: decisions already made by their owners, with the review date. Nobody debates them. The second is the short list that genuinely needs the room: irreversible, high exposure, or cross-functional. Those get the time that used to be spent re-approving the small ones.

Teams that make this split report the same thing. Fewer meetings, faster small decisions, and the large ones finally get the scrutiny they deserved all along.

## A question for the next leadership meeting

Which decisions wait for approval even when the downside is limited? Make that list. Then give each one a route.
