**Core message:** "You don't need to go viral. You need a system." Going viral once is luck;
doing it every month is strategy. Replace random posting with templates, content pillars,
storytelling patterns, and platform-specific playbooks so virality is repeatable.

**Target customer:** founders, agencies, coaches, and business owners doing roughly $1M to $50M
a year who want leads from short-form video and are tired of bloated marketing funnels.
Explicitly *not* beginners or side-hustlers (third-party reviews confirm the fit).

## 3. The funnel (what they actually sell and in what order)

```
Organic short-form (TikTok / Reels / Shorts, founder-led)
        │
        ▼
Paid ads (retarget warm viewers; promote proven organic winners)
        │
        ▼
Free Skool community "Viral Coach Training Material" (~30k members)
  + free training / lead magnet
        │
        ▼
"Schedule a free call with our team to see if you qualify"
  (AI-powered SMS follow-up qualifies and books the call automatically)
        │
        ▼
Sales call → high-ticket tiers
```

**Offer ladder (prices from third-party reviews, not confirmed on-site):**

| Tier | What's in it | Price signal |
|---|---|---|
| Free community | Skool group, free training, weekly content | $0 |
| Core program | Video training, 1:1 strategy sessions, paid Skool community, weekly coaching calls | from ~$6,000 (often quoted as $6k/yr) |
| Done-with-you | 12-mo Skool + 6-mo Slack 1:1, custom plan, studio setup, scriptwriting, interview-style content sessions, gear recs, Vista Social posting, ManyChat funnels, unlimited coaching calls | mid five figures |
| Enterprise consulting | Large-scale business clients | reported $50k to $199k |

Key sales insight from Iles himself: **cold audiences buy "organic growth," so that is what the
front end sells.** Once inside, the team diagnoses what the client actually needs (offer,
messaging, website, automations, paid ads) and expands. He says if they had led with "paid
ads" they would never have won the business. Paid ads are introduced only after a client has a
video that already earned trust organically, at which point turning it into an ad is a
"no-brainer."

## 4. Advertising and content strategy — what makes them "dialed in"

### 4a. Creative principles they teach and (by all accounts) run themselves
1. **Win the first 3 seconds.** A "Hook Library" of scroll-stopping opening lines is a core
   deliverable. Hook psychology is module one, not an afterthought.
2. **Founder-led talking head.** Daniel is the face. Low-production, direct-to-camera, real
   over polished.
3. **Micro-storytelling.** Deliver a transformation inside seconds. Hook → body → close.
4. **Content pillars.** A small set of recurring themes that reinforce authority so every
   post compounds brand recognition.
5. **"Trust onion."** Layered trust-building across the short-form funnel: the viewer moves
   from stranger → follower → community member → call → client, with each layer earning the
   next ask.
6. **Visual consistency.** Same framing, captions, and look so the feed reads as one brand.
7. **Clear CTA every time**, usually to the free community or the free call.
8. **Platform-specific, cross-posted.** One shoot, cut for TikTok, Reels, Shorts, scheduled
   via Vista Social.
9. **AI-assisted scripting.** Their proprietary AI drafts scripts and ad copy; humans refine.
10. **Plan the "second wave" before the first viral hit.** Lead magnet, welcome sequence, and
    intro offer are built so a spike converts into subscribers and calls instead of vanity views.

### 4b. Paid media playbook (from their Meta ads masterclass modules)
- Campaign structure and precise lookalike audiences
- **Pool social proof:** run ads from a single post ID so likes/comments accumulate on one
  creative instead of fragmenting across ad sets
- Dedicated **retargeting audience** built from video viewers and engagers
- Promote organic videos that have already proven out (earned trust, then paid reach)
- Account-ban avoidance and compliance hygiene
- Service-business optimisation: the pixel does not fire on a purchase like ecommerce, so
  optimise for calls/qualified leads and accept a slower feedback loop
- AI SMS follow-up to qualify and book leads automatically after opt-in

### 4c. Proof and authority mechanics
- Big-number proof stack repeated verbatim everywhere (views, leads, clients, industries, logos)
- Volume of third-party reviews (Clutch, Trustpilot, Google) used as a trust layer
- Press placements (Hype Magazine, Whop founder story, podcast circuit) that retell the
  origin story
- Free community size (30k) displayed as social proof for the paid tier

## 5. What to copy when we build our ads

- **One-line proof stack.** Pick 3 to 5 numbers and one or two recognisable names, repeat
  them identically on every surface until they are furniture.
- **Lead with the outcome the cold audience already wants**, not the mechanism we think they
  need. Diagnose and expand after they are in.
- **Hook library before anything else.** Write 30+ openers, test them organically, promote
  winners as ads.
- **Founder-led, phone-shot, captioned.** Match the feed, don't interrupt it.
- **Free community as the top of funnel.** Low-friction opt-in that doubles as social proof.
- **"See if you qualify" CTA.** Frames the call as selective, raises perceived value, and
  pre-filters.
- **Automated SMS/DM follow-up** between opt-in and call.
- **Organic → paid promotion of proven winners** instead of cold creative testing on paid
  budget.
- **Pooled social proof on ads** (single post ID).
- **Build the second-wave assets** (lead magnet, welcome sequence, intro offer) before
  spending.

## 6. What to be careful about (from reviews)

Third-party review sites (ScamRisk, MarketingAgencyReviews, Trustpilot/Reddit summaries)
cluster complaints around: high-pressure sales calls, undisclosed pricing, plateaued growth
after the first weeks, lack of personalisation at scale, and refund friction despite an
advertised 30-day guarantee with conditions. Positive reviews praise clarity, structure,
onboarding, and community. Lesson for us: copy the acquisition machine, but publish pricing
or at least a floor, keep the guarantee simple, and make personalisation visible so the
back-end matches the front-end promise.
