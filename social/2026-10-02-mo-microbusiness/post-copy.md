## LinkedIn (CannaShark Consulting company page; also share from Adrian's profile)

The rules reach the contract. None of them reach the wait.

Missouri drew its final round of marijuana microbusiness applicants on Sept. 9: 888 applications, roughly 77 licenses issuing from December. The rules the state put in place in May are good ones. They define what it means to majority-own and operate a license, let regulators review ownership arrangements before a license issues rather than after, and require eligible owners to deal with the division directly.

What they cannot reach is the months between a winning draw and an open door. Forty-six licenses from the first two rounds are active but not yet approved to operate. As of Aug. 18, only 21 microbusinesses were open. That gap is where someone offers to carry the license for you, and where the terms matter more than the merit.

I am a consultant, so I wrote the uncomfortable version of that story for the Missouri Independent, including the clauses to watch for from people in my own industry: equity that vests on signature rather than delivery, a management agreement wearing a consulting label, fees calculated on gross rather than net, and SOPs that describe a building you do not have.

Thanks to Jason Hancock and the Independent's commentary desk for the space, and to Rebecca Rivas, whose reporting on this beat made the piece possible.
